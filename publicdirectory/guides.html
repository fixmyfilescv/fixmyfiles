Which format should you use?

  • PNG vs JPG Format comparison

    PNG vs JPG: which should you use?

    Use JPG for photographs and PNG for everything with sharp edges: screenshots, logos, diagrams and text. PNG never loses detail but makes photos several times larger; JPG keeps photos small but smudges lines and can’t be transparent. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • WEBP vs PNG Format comparison

    WEBP vs PNG: which should you use?

    For images on a website, WebP does everything PNG does, transparency included, in a noticeably smaller file. PNG is still the safer choice for files you send to other people or open in older software. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • WEBP vs JPG Format comparison

    WEBP vs JPG: which should you use?

    WebP makes photos about a quarter to a third smaller than JPG at the same visible quality, and every current browser shows it. JPG remains the format every upload form, printer and email client accepts without question. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • AVIF vs WEBP Format comparison

    AVIF vs WEBP: which should you use?

    AVIF usually produces the smallest file of any image format at the same quality, especially for photos, but it is slower to encode and slightly less widely supported. WebP is the practical middle ground: small, fast and supported everywhere modern. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • HEIC vs JPG Format comparison

    HEIC vs JPG: which should you use?

    HEIC stores iPhone photos in about half the space of JPG with the same quality, which is why Apple uses it. The catch is compatibility: Windows, Android apps and most upload forms still expect JPG, so convert when a photo leaves your Apple devices. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • SVG vs PNG Format comparison

    SVG vs PNG: which should you use?

    SVG is drawn from shapes, so logos and icons stay razor sharp at any size in a tiny file. PNG is a grid of pixels that works for any picture, including photos and screenshots, and opens in every app. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • TIFF vs PNG Format comparison

    TIFF vs PNG: which should you use?

    Both are lossless. TIFF is built for print, scanning and professional photo work — 16-bit color, CMYK, layers and many pages in one file. PNG is built for screens: every browser and app opens it, it keeps transparency, and it is usually smaller for screenshots and graphics. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • AVIF vs JPG Format comparison

    AVIF vs JPG: which should you use?

    AVIF makes photos much smaller than JPG at the same visible quality and handles HDR and transparency, and every current browser shows it. JPG remains the format every upload form, printer, email client and photo app accepts, so use AVIF on your own site and JPG everywhere else. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • GIF vs WEBP Format comparison

    GIF vs WEBP: which should you use?

    Animated WebP does everything a GIF does in a fraction of the size, with full color instead of GIF’s 256 and smooth transparency. GIF’s advantage is reach: it animates in email, in old apps and in chat tools that still show a WebP as a still or refuse it. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026

Which format should you use?

  • MP4 vs MOV Format comparison

    MP4 vs MOV: which should you use?

    MP4 and MOV usually contain exactly the same video, so quality isn’t the difference. MP4 plays on everything and is what websites and apps expect; MOV is Apple’s format, best kept for editing in Final Cut or iMovie. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • MP4 vs MKV Format comparison

    MP4 vs MKV: which should you use?

    MKV can hold almost anything, including several audio languages and subtitle tracks, which makes it ideal for a media library. MP4 holds less but plays on phones, browsers, editors and social apps, so it wins whenever a video is shared. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • MP4 vs WEBM Format comparison

    MP4 vs WEBM: which should you use?

    WebM is the open format made for the web, and VP9 or AV1 inside it can be smaller than H.264 in MP4. MP4 is still the more compatible choice: it plays on iPhones, TVs and in every editor and social app. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • AVI vs MP4 Format comparison

    AVI vs MP4: which should you use?

    AVI is a 1990s container that usually holds older, less efficient codecs, so the same video is often much larger and won’t play on phones or in browsers. Convert AVI to MP4 unless an old device or program specifically needs AVI. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • MKV vs AVI Format comparison

    MKV vs AVI: which should you use?

    MKV is the modern choice: it holds any codec, several audio tracks, subtitles and chapters, and handles HD video properly. AVI dates from 1992 and is tied to older codecs like DivX and Xvid, so the only reason to keep one is an old DVD player or TV that reads nothing else. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • WMV vs MP4 Format comparison

    WMV vs MP4: which should you use?

    WMV is Microsoft’s older format: it plays in Windows Media Player but struggles on Macs, iPhones, Android and the web. MP4 plays everywhere, from phones to smart TVs to every browser, usually in a smaller file, so convert WMV to MP4 whenever it leaves an old Windows PC. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026

Which format should you use?

  • MP3 vs WAV Format comparison

    MP3 vs WAV: which should you use?

    WAV is uncompressed, perfect audio about ten times the size of an MP3. MP3 at 192 kbps or more sounds the same to almost everyone, so use it for listening and sharing, and WAV for recording, editing and mastering. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • M4A vs MP3 Format comparison

    M4A vs MP3: which should you use?

    M4A (AAC audio) sounds a little better than MP3 at the same bitrate and is the default for iPhones and iTunes. MP3 is older and slightly less efficient, but every car stereo, speaker and player ever made can play it. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • FLAC vs MP3 Format comparison

    FLAC vs MP3: which should you use?

    FLAC is lossless: it keeps CD quality in roughly half the space of WAV. MP3 throws detail away to be about a fifth of FLAC’s size. Keep FLAC as your archive and convert to MP3 for phones, cars and sharing. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • FLAC vs WAV Format comparison

    FLAC vs WAV: which should you use?

    FLAC and WAV sound identical, bit for bit. FLAC is compressed without loss to about half the size and carries proper tags and artwork; WAV is uncompressed and opens in every audio editor. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • OGG vs MP3 Format comparison

    OGG vs MP3: which should you use?

    Ogg Vorbis is an open format that sounds a little better than MP3 at the same bitrate and is common in games and on Android. MP3 plays on far more devices, so it is the safer choice for anything you share. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • OPUS vs MP3 Format comparison

    OPUS vs MP3: which should you use?

    Opus sounds better than MP3 at the same size, and far better at the low bitrates used for voice: it is what WhatsApp voice notes, Discord and video calls use. MP3 is still the format every car stereo, old player and DJ program opens without question. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
  • AIFF vs WAV Format comparison

    AIFF vs WAV: which should you use?

    AIFF and WAV hold exactly the same uncompressed audio, so they sound identical and are about the same size. AIFF is Apple’s and keeps song tags neatly in Music and Logic; WAV is the Windows and broadcast standard that every program on every system opens. By Suprabhat Sep 19, 2026
